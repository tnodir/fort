# Configuration model details

Moved out of `AGENTS.md`: read it when working on this area.

## IP Addresses

The "IP Addresses" tab's lists are the two `AddressGroup`s (`FirewallConf::addressGroups()`: include/exclude texts and zones, the Include/Exclude All flags; the driver gets them as `FORT_CONF_ADDR_GROUP`s, checked by `fort_conf_conn_filter_flags_filtered()`). The first one (`lanAddressGroup()`)'s exclude list is the "Local Network Addresses" (a remote address not included by it is LAN: `conn->is_local_net`); the second one (`inetAddressGroup()`)'s exclude list is the Internet's "Block Addresses" (reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_IP_BLOCK_INET`) and its include list the "Allow Addresses" (allowed for any program, after the blocked ones: `FORT_CONN_REASON_IP_ALLOW_INET`; as with the Filter Disabled, `fort_conf_conn_flags_filtered()` returns FALSE for it, so the App's check collects its traffic and logs a new App). The UI doesn't show their Include/Exclude All flags: the DB migration to v68 set the second one's Include All and cleared its old hidden "Include" list; the other flags stay as the old UI set them (e.g. the first one's Include All may be unset). The Internet box's "Filter Fort Firewall's Service" (`FirewallConf::filterFortService()`, off by default) makes the driver filter the Service's connections as the other programs' ones, else they are allowed (`FORT_CONN_REASON_FORT_SERVICE`) after the flags and the address groups (`fort_conf_conn_app_allowed()`: the traffic is collected; its unknown exe is logged as a new App allowed, without an alert: the App's log record has `FORT_LOG_FLAG_OPT_ALERTED`): the driver keeps the process which opened the device (`fort_device()->client_process_id`) and marks its connections by `conn->is_fort_service`; the Filter Simulator's connection is the Service's by its "Fort Firewall's Service" flag (the `simulate` command's `service=1`; a connection of the Connections window by its reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_FORT_SERVICE`, the log has no other mark of it). Without the Service the device's client is the UI process: `ConfData::writeConfFlags()` sets `filter_fort_service` then by `ConfDriverState::isService` (`ConfManager::driverConfState()`: the driver's conf values not kept in `FirewallConf`, with the active Time Periods' mask), and the tab shows a warning under the flag (`OptBasePage::createNoServiceWarning()`, as the DNS tab over its options). `AddressesPage` shows each list by an `AddressesArea` (`form/opt/pages/addresses/`) of its `FirewallConf::AddressList` (the groups' include and exclude lists in turn: `FirewallConf::addressListZones()`/`addressListText()`, also naming the list in a parse error).

## A Program's Network Filters and Rules

A Program's Network Filters are rule text lines in `app.filters_text`, edited by `FilterEditDialog` (`form/rule/`; the Edit Rule dialog uses it too, to append a line to the Rule's text). Its fields build the read-only line by `FilterLineText` (`util/conf/filterlinetext.h`: named sections, e.g. `IP(1.1.1.1):Port(80):Act(Block)`; the Host and Remote IP fields' lines are kept as written, with their `#` comments, on their own lines inside the brackets, and shown again by `RuleFilter::valuesText` (`FilterLine::valuesLines()`; a field of comments only is no filter: the dialog shows it empty and drops it on saving), the Network Filters' list shows such a filter by its lines; also by a connection's `Conn`, for the Connections windows' "Add Filter" and "Copy as Filter"; the common window's "Add Filter" opens the Program's form on the Network Filters tab, a blocked Program's on the General tab till the filter is saved), the dialog gets one; `FilterLine` (`util/conf/filterline.h`) holds it and parses it by its `RuleTextParser`, giving the `RuleFilter`s by type to fill the fields. The Program's Terminating Rule (on the form's Rules tab: `TerminatingRuleSelector`, as in the Edit Rule dialog; Allow, Block or Drop, with an alert) is the last line, after the `# Terminating` comment line, e.g. `Act(Block):Opt(Alert)` (`ConfUtil::parseAppFiltersText()` / `appFiltersText()` give the text's `Rule`: the filters' lines and the Terminating flags; the Network Filters and Rules tabs' pages each replace their part of the text). The text's filters (and the `# Terminating` line) are separated by the `;` line (`"\n;\n"`, `ConfUtil::splitAppFilters()` / `joinAppFilters()`), as a filter may be of several lines: the parser takes `;` as a new line, so the whole text is a rule text; `FilterEditDialog` rejects a text with the `;` line or of several lines of filters (`FilterLine::isSingleLine()`); `prog set-filters` takes a file's text as is (trimmed): without the `;` lines it's one filter; the DB migration to v72 added the `;` lines. The Program's Rules (App and Preset Rules) are `app.ruleSet`, the conf DB's `app_rule` rows by `order_index` (read by `group_concat()`, `ConfUtil::parseRuleSetIds()`; a deleted Rule's rows are deleted), edited on the form's Rules tab (`ProgRulesPage`) by the `RuleSetArea` of the Edit Rule dialog's Presets tab; the DB migration to v71 moved the old single `app.rule_id` there. The driver gets them as a `FORT_CONF_RULE` (without zones and Time Period; its set is the Program's Rules) after the path in the app entry (`app_entry.rule_size`, so ADDAPP updates them) and checks the filters, then the Rules, then the Terminating Rule (reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_PROGRAM_FILTER`, or `FORT_CONN_REASON_RULE` with the id of the set's Rule which decided); the rule is checked without the conf's lock (`conn->app.rule`), so an exe entry with a rule is never changed in place nor freed before the conf (`fort_conf_ref_exe_free_entry()`), and its set under the Rules' lock (`fort_devconf_app_rule_conn_filtered()`: a rule without a set doesn't lock).

The driver doesn't check the filters and Rules of a Program with the Block or Kill Process action, and its form's Network Filters and Rules tabs are disabled. A filter saved for it from the Connections windows' "Add Filter" (`ProgMainPage::onFilterSaved()`) switches the form to Allow with the blocking Terminating Rule. The Program's other settings stay and are checked before the filters (`fort_conf_conn_app_filtered()`): its Groups' Rules and Zones may allow more than the filters; they, Block In/Out, LAN Only and a disabled Group may block a filter's address.

## Time Periods

Groups, Speed Limits and Rules may refer to a `TimePeriod` (`conf/timeperiod.h`, max 64: a list of intervals, each with its week days and time from/to) by `period_id`, applied while their `period_enabled` is set. `ConfTimePeriodManager` tracks the Time Periods' activity by minutes. For the Groups and Speed Limits it is folded into their enabled masks written to the driver (`writeGroupFlags()` / `writeSpeedLimitFlags()`). The Rules are checked by the driver: a rule's `period_id` (0 - always active) against the active Time Periods' mask, sent in `FORT_CONF_IO.periods` with `SETCONF` and updated by `SETPERIODS` on the activity's change.

## Hosts Zones

A Zone of the Hosts type (`host` in `conf/zone/types.json`, the `host_text` and `host_file` sources) lists host names, parsed by `HostRange` (`util/net/`): `example.com` matches the name itself only, `*.example.com` its subdomains of any depth but not itself, `.example.com` both (kept as the two names); a name with other masks is matched by `HostPattern` (`ads*.example.com`, `google.*`, `*telemetry*`: `*` is any characters of a label, the leading and trailing ones also any labels before and after it; each of them is checked for every name, so a list has at most `HostRange::patternNamesMax` of them); the names are compared case-insensitively, without the root's trailing dot, an internationalized name is converted to Punycode (`QUrl::toAce()`). A Zone source with `subdomains` in `sources.json` (the lists of names, e.g. OISD; the hosts files, e.g. StevenBlack, of the `hosts_file` Zone type: its pattern takes the name after the address, but not the file's own `localhost`, `local`, `broadcasthost`, `ip6-…` and `0.0.0.0`) takes each name and `*.` wildcard as the `.` name, the name with its subdomains, as such lists mean it: `HostRange::setWithSubdomains()` on the cache's loading (`TaskZoneDownloader::loadHosts()`), so the cache file doesn't depend on the flag. `TaskZoneDownloader` keeps the normalized names in the Zone's cache file and gives the driver an empty address list, so the Zone is in the driver's `FORT_CONF_ZONES.mask` and `enabled_mask` like the others. Its addresses are added at runtime by `ADDHOSTIPS` (`FORT_CONF_HOST_IPS`: the IPs of a DNS answer with its TTL, to be sent by the service's DNS forwarder): the driver keeps them in `FORT_HOST_IPS` (`fortcnf_host.c`, max 65535 IPs, the expired ones removed when it's full, at most once a minute: the purge walks all IPs under the lock; an IP has one expiration time for all its Zones). An expired IP isn't matched by a new connection, but is matched by an established connection's reauthorization (`conn->is_reauth`, on any conf's change) for a day more (`FORT_HOST_IP_REAUTH_TIME`), as the connections outlive the TTL; it's removed only then. An expired IP added again keeps its old Zones, for the new connections too, till it expires for the reauthorization (as the IP's Zones share its expiration time anyway). The ALE callout looks up the remote IP's Zones once into `conn->host_zones_mask`, which `fort_conf_zones_ip_included()` checks before the Zones' address lists (the Filter Simulator has no such IPs). `SETZONES` removes the IPs' Zones not in its mask, as a removed Zone's id can be reused (`FortManager` writes the Zones on `zoneRemoved`); the client's disconnect doesn't clear the IPs: they stay till their TTL, as the DNS clients' caches keep them.

The Hosts Zones' names are matched in a DB (`HostZonesDb`, `ui/dns/`: `hosts.db` in the cache folder, `FortSettings::hostZonesFilePath()`; opened while the forwarder runs), filled from the Zones' cache files and recreated on its version's change. The table `host(name, name_mask, sub_mask)` has a row per name with the Zones listing the name itself and the Zones listing its subdomains (a name with its subdomains is one row); `host_pattern` has the names with other masks, kept in memory too (`HostPattern`); `host_zone` has each Zone's cache file's checksum (`bin_checksum`) and the `subdomains` flag its names were loaded with. `TaskInfoZoneDownloader::syncHostZones()` rewrites only the Zones whose checksum or flag differ (`HostZonesDb::setZone()`, in one transaction) and removes the Zones that aren't the Hosts ones anymore; a disabled Zone's names stay: `match()` returns the enabled Zones (`setEnabledMask()`) of the mask it's given, without a lookup when none of them is. A name's lookups (the name and its parent domains) run in one read transaction: a statement with a transaction of its own takes several times longer. After a write the DB's page cache is released (`PRAGMA shrink_memory`: it kept ~18 MB after filling 490K names). A test or a tool using the DB needs `Q_INIT_RESOURCE(dns_migrations)`: without the migrations' resource the DB opens with no tables.

The service's DNS forwarder (`ui/dns/`: `DnsManager`, over the `FortFirewallDnsLib` of `src/dns/`; only with `isService()`, by the ini's `dns/filterEnabled`, off by default, the Options window's DNS tab) listens on `127.0.0.1:53` and `[::1]:53`, UDP and TCP (`DnsListener`). While it runs, a rule of the Name Resolution Policy Table (a key of the Dnscache's `Parameters\DnsPolicyConfig`: the namespace `.` to `127.0.0.1`) sends the Dnscache's queries to it; the rule is removed on its stop, also on the service's start with the option off (e.g. after a crash), and the c-ares sockets don't go through the Dnscache. While it runs, the ini's `dns/blockPorts` (the DNS tab's "Block ports 53 and 853 for other programs", on by default) makes the driver block the outbound UDP and TCP connections to the remote port 53 (`FORT_CONN_REASON_BLOCK_DNS`) and to the port 853 of DNS over TLS and over QUIC (`FORT_CONN_REASON_BLOCK_DOT`) of the programs other than the Service and of the non-loopback addresses (`block_dns_ports` by `ConfDriverState::dnsPortsBlocked`, set by `ConfManager::setDnsPortsBlocked()`, which writes the driver's flags on its change; `fort_conf_conn_dns_blocked()` after Block All, before the LAN and Internet checks), so they can't resolve the names past the forwarder by these protocols; DNS over HTTPS (port 443 of the web sites, e.g. the browsers' Secure DNS) isn't blocked and bypasses it, as the DNS tab's tooltips say. A query's name listed by an enabled Hosts Zone of the ini's `dns/blockZones` (the DNS tab's "Block Zones": a `HostZonesSelector`, the `ZonesSelector` listing only the Hosts Zones by `MaskSelector::listedMask()`: `ZoneListModel::hostZonesMask()`, by one query; the master clears the bit of a removed Zone, as its id can be reused, and of a Zone whose new source isn't of a Hosts type: `ConfZoneManager::updateDnsBlockZones()`, saved by `ConfManager::saveIniChanged()`, which notifies the clients as the Options window's save does) gets `NXDOMAIN` (with a SOA record for the negative caching) without asking the upstream, even if a `host()` filter lists it (`DnsHostBlocker::isQueryBlocked()`), and instead of the upstream's answer if they list it since the query (`DnsHostBlocker::isAnswerBlocked()`). The other queries go to the upstream servers of the ini's `dns/servers` (a string list) by the resolver of their protocol (`DnsResolver::create()`, created on the forwarder's start): `DnsPlainResolver` asks the servers' addresses, else the adapters' DNS servers, which c-ares re-reads on the network's changes, by a c-ares channel on its own event thread, `DnsHttpsResolver` the DNS over HTTPS ones (see below); its answer's A/AAAA addresses and the `ipv4hint`/`ipv6hint` ones of its HTTPS/SVCB records (RFC 9460; of the query's name, as a CNAME's addresses, whatever the record's target) are added to the Hosts Zones referenced by the driver's conf (`HostRefs::zonesMask()`, see `host()` below: the address groups', Programs' and Rules' Zones; the driver doesn't know the Zones' types, so another Zone's IPs would only fill its table) and the `host()` filters, which list the query's name (`DnsManager::matchHost()`, by the answer: they may be changed since the query; the Zones' names in the DB, synced with the Zones' cache files on `zoneUpdated` and `TaskManager::zonesUpdated`, a removed Zone's names removed at once on `zoneRemoved`, and the `host()` filters' names in a `HostMatcher`, rebuilt from `ConfManager::hostRefs()` at once on its `hostRefsChanged`) by `ADDHOSTIPS` (their max TTL plus a margin of 60 s: the programs connect after the answer, a TTL may be 0) before the answer is sent to the client (`SERVFAIL` if the driver fails). The Dnscache's answers bypass the forwarder: they are flushed (`DnsUtil::flushResolverCache()`) on the forwarder's start, the Block Zones' and the Zones' names' changes and when a new `host()` filter or an enabled Hosts Zone is referenced (`DnsManager::updateHostRefs()`: by `HostRefs::lastHostId()`, as the ids grow, and the referenced Zones' mask; a removed reference's names only don't add their IPs anymore), so the names are resolved anew, adding their IPs (e.g. of the `host()` filters' new ids after the service's restart), and the blocked ones aren't found. The answer is built on the c-ares thread (`DnsAnswer`: the client's id, the upstream's EDNS cookie removed, truncated with `TC` over the client's UDP size) and passed to the service's thread by a queued call. A query from a loopback address with the id and question (the name, type and class) of a query the resolver sent and waits for (`ares_send_dnsrec()`'s qid; c-ares's own cache is off, as a cached answer has no qid) is a loop (the adapters' DNS includes the listener) and gets `SERVFAIL`.

The DNS over HTTPS servers (RFC 8484; `DnsHttpsServer`, `DnsHttpsResolver` of `src/dns/`: a `dns/servers` list of the `https://` URLs; a list mixing them with the plain addresses isn't used: `DnsHttpsServer::isMixedServers()`, `DnsResolver::create()` gives null, the forwarder doesn't start, the DNS tab warns under the servers' field) are URLs with the server's address: as the host, or as the host name's `ip` parameter (`https://dns.example/dns-query?ip=192.0.2.1`, as in Portmaster). A name without the address is refused: the system's resolver would ask the forwarder itself. The query is posted (`application/dns-message`, with the id 0) by `QNetworkAccessManager` to the URL by the address, with the host name as `QNetworkRequest::setPeerVerifyName()`: Schannel sends it as the SNI and verifies the certificate by it, while the `Host` header (HTTP/2's `:authority`) is the address. HTTP/2 needs ALPN (Windows 8.1+): Quad9 and Applied Privacy answer 505 to HTTP/1.1. The servers are asked in the list's order: the next one on an error (of the network or HTTP, a timeout of 4 s, not a DNS response), `SERVFAIL` after the last one, without asking the adapters' servers; a failed server is asked after the other ones for 5 minutes. A redirect isn't followed (its host name would be resolved by the forwarder), the cookies aren't kept. `DnsTest.httpsResolver` asks local HTTP servers (the resolver's `setServers()`).

The DNS tab's combo box sets the servers of a DNS provider (`DnsProvider`, `ui/dns/data/providers.json`: the providers of Portmaster's settings by their DNS over HTTPS URLs) into the servers' field (a server per line; its text is split by `DnsUtil::splitServers()`: by commas, spaces or new lines), which is the option's value: the combo box's index follows the field's servers (0 for the adapters' servers of the empty text, -1 with the "Custom" placeholder for the servers of no provider). After changing the list ask each of its servers by `DnsTest.DISABLED_providersServers` (`DnsTest.exe --gtest_also_run_disabled_tests --gtest_filter=*providersServers`: it needs the network, so it's run only manually). The tab's Test button (only with the service) asks each server of the field for `example.com` by `DnsServersTest` (`src/dns/`: a resolver of the server's protocol per server, in parallel, for 5 s at most; with an invalid server none is asked, with the mixed protocols none is asked and each server's result is `StatusMixed`) on the service: a client's `DnsManagerRpc::createServersTest()` gives a test started by `Rpc_DnsManager_testServers`, each server's result is sent back to the client which started it by `Rpc_DnsManager_serverTested`; the results are shown under the field as they come. The control command `dns test` (`ControlCommandDns`) tests the ini's servers likewise: it sends its result when the test finishes (`ProcessCommandResult::isResultDeferred`, `ControlCommandManager::sendResult()`), the client waits for it longer (`ControlCommandBase::resultWaitMsecs()`); a server not answering NOERROR makes it an error.

A rule text's `host(example.com, *.cdn.net)` filter (`FORT_RULE_FILTER_TYPE_HOST`; its names as in `HostRange`; an address type: `host(a.com):443` is its port) matches the remote IPs of its names' DNS answers. Each set of names of the Rules' and the Programs' filters (`HostRefs::namesKey()`: the sorted normalized names) has an id, kept by the master's `ConfManager::hostRefs()`: `ConfBuffer` with the ids (`setHostRefs()`) gives the new names the next id while writing the Rules (`SETRULES`), the Programs (`SETCONF`) or a Program (`ADDAPP`), and the writes forget the ids of the names neither the Rules nor the Programs have (`HostRefs::Source`; a Program's names and Zones are kept by its path, as the driver keeps the Programs: its `ADDAPP` replaces them, of its deletion too); without them (validation, the Filter Simulator) the buffer has its own, which nothing matches: the Filter Simulator has no `host_ip_included` callback. Likewise `HostRefs::zonesMask()` has the Zones the driver's conf references: the address groups' include and exclude Zones and the Programs' Zones of `SETCONF` and `ADDAPP`, the Rules' Zones of `SETRULES` (`HostRefs::addZones()`); a change of the ids or the Zones emits `ConfManager::hostRefsChanged`. The ids aren't reused, so a removed filter's IPs don't match a new one; the driver removes the `host()` filters' IPs on the device's open, as a new client's ids are new (after the service's restart the IPs are added again by the new answers, the Dnscache flushed). `ADDHOSTIPS` adds the IPs to the Zones and to the ids (`FORT_CONF_HOST_IPS.host_n` ids before the IPs): `FORT_HOST_IPS` keeps a `host()` filter's IP as the IP and `host_id` entry with its own expiration time (the Zones' IP has `host_id` 0), which the filter's check looks up by the connection's `host_ip_included` callback under the table's lock (the Filter Simulator has no callback).
