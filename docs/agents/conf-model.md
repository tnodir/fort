# Configuration model details

Moved out of `AGENTS.md`: read it when working on this area.

## IP Addresses

The "IP Addresses" tab's lists are the two `AddressGroup`s (`FirewallConf::addressGroups()`: include/exclude texts and zones, the Include/Exclude All flags; the driver gets them as `FORT_CONF_ADDR_GROUP`s, checked by `fort_conf_conn_filter_flags_filtered()`). The first one (`lanAddressGroup()`)'s exclude list is the "Local Network Addresses" (a remote address not included by it is LAN: `conn->is_local_net`); the second one (`inetAddressGroup()`)'s exclude list is the Internet's "Block Addresses" (reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_IP_BLOCK_INET`) and its include list the "Allow Addresses" (allowed for any program, after the blocked ones: `FORT_CONN_REASON_IP_ALLOW_INET`; as with the Filter Disabled, `fort_conf_conn_flags_filtered()` returns FALSE for it, so the App's check collects its traffic and logs a new App). The UI doesn't show their Include/Exclude All flags: the DB migration to v68 set the second one's Include All and cleared its old hidden "Include" list; the other flags stay as the old UI set them (e.g. the first one's Include All may be unset). `AddressesPage` shows each list by an `AddressesArea` (`form/opt/pages/addresses/`) of its `FirewallConf::AddressList` (the groups' include and exclude lists in turn: `FirewallConf::addressListZones()`/`addressListText()`, also naming the list in a parse error).

## A Program's Network Filters and Rules

A Program's Network Filters are rule text lines in `app.filters_text`, edited by `FilterEditDialog` (`form/rule/`; the Edit Rule dialog uses it too, to append a line to the Rule's text). Its fields build the read-only line by `FilterLineText` (`util/conf/filterlinetext.h`: named sections, e.g. `IP(1.1.1.1):Port(80):Act(Block)`; the Host and Remote IP fields' lines are kept as written, with their `#` comments, on their own lines inside the brackets, and shown again by `RuleFilter::valuesText` (`FilterLine::valuesLines()`; a field of comments only is no filter: the dialog shows it empty and drops it on saving), the Network Filters' list shows such a filter by its lines; also by a connection's `Conn`, for the Connections windows' "Add Filter" and "Copy as Filter"; the common window's "Add Filter" opens the Program's form on the Network Filters tab, a blocked Program's on the General tab till the filter is saved), the dialog gets one; `FilterLine` (`util/conf/filterline.h`) holds it and parses it by its `RuleTextParser`, giving the `RuleFilter`s by type to fill the fields. The Program's Terminating Rule (on the form's Rules tab: `TerminatingRuleSelector`, as in the Edit Rule dialog; Allow, Block or Drop, with an alert) is the last line, after the `# Terminating` comment line, e.g. `Act(Block):Opt(Alert)` (`ConfUtil::parseAppFiltersText()` / `appFiltersText()` give the text's `Rule`: the filters' lines and the Terminating flags; the Network Filters and Rules tabs' pages each replace their part of the text). The text's filters (and the `# Terminating` line) are separated by the `;` line (`"\n;\n"`, `ConfUtil::splitAppFilters()` / `joinAppFilters()`), as a filter may be of several lines: the parser takes `;` as a new line, so the whole text is a rule text; `FilterEditDialog` rejects a text with the `;` line or of several lines of filters (`FilterLine::isSingleLine()`); `prog set-filters` takes a file's text as is (trimmed): without the `;` lines it's one filter; the DB migration to v72 added the `;` lines. The Program's Rules (App and Preset Rules) are `app.ruleSet`, the conf DB's `app_rule` rows by `order_index` (read by `group_concat()`, `ConfUtil::parseRuleSetIds()`; a deleted Rule's rows are deleted), edited on the form's Rules tab (`ProgRulesPage`) by the `RuleSetArea` of the Edit Rule dialog's Presets tab; the DB migration to v71 moved the old single `app.rule_id` there. The driver gets them as a `FORT_CONF_RULE` (without zones and Time Period; its set is the Program's Rules) after the path in the app entry (`app_entry.rule_size`, so ADDAPP updates them) and checks the filters, then the Rules, then the Terminating Rule (reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_PROGRAM_FILTER`, or `FORT_CONN_REASON_RULE` with the id of the set's Rule which decided); the rule is checked without the conf's lock (`conn->app.rule`), so an exe entry with a rule is never changed in place nor freed before the conf (`fort_conf_ref_exe_free_entry()`), and its set under the Rules' lock (`fort_devconf_app_rule_conn_filtered()`: a rule without a set doesn't lock).

The driver doesn't check the filters and Rules of a Program with the Block or Kill Process action, and its form's Network Filters and Rules tabs are disabled. A filter saved for it from the Connections windows' "Add Filter" (`ProgMainPage::onFilterSaved()`) switches the form to Allow with the blocking Terminating Rule. The Program's other settings stay and are checked before the filters (`fort_conf_conn_app_filtered()`): its Groups' Rules and Zones may allow more than the filters; they, Block In/Out, LAN Only and a disabled Group may block a filter's address.

## Time Periods

Groups, Speed Limits and Rules may refer to a `TimePeriod` (`conf/timeperiod.h`, max 64: a list of intervals, each with its week days and time from/to) by `period_id`, applied while their `period_enabled` is set. `ConfTimePeriodManager` tracks the Time Periods' activity by minutes. For the Groups and Speed Limits it is folded into their enabled masks written to the driver (`writeGroupFlags()` / `writeSpeedLimitFlags()`). The Rules are checked by the driver: a rule's `period_id` (0 - always active) against the active Time Periods' mask, sent in `FORT_CONF_IO.periods` with `SETCONF` and updated by `SETPERIODS` on the activity's change.

## Hosts Zones

A Zone of the Hosts type (`host` in `conf/zone/types.json`, the `host_text` and `host_file` sources) lists host names, parsed by `HostRange` (`util/net/`): `example.com` matches the name itself only, `*.example.com` its subdomains of any depth but not itself, `.example.com` both (kept as the two names); a name with other masks is matched by `HostPattern` (`ads*.example.com`, `google.*`, `*telemetry*`: `*` is any characters of a label, the leading and trailing ones also any labels before and after it; each of them is checked for every name, so a list has at most `HostRange::patternNamesMax` of them); the names are compared case-insensitively, without the root's trailing dot, an internationalized name is converted to Punycode (`QUrl::toAce()`). A Zone source with `subdomains` in `sources.json` (the lists of names, e.g. OISD; the hosts files, e.g. StevenBlack, of the `hosts_file` Zone type: its pattern takes the name after the address, but not the file's own `localhost`, `local`, `broadcasthost`, `ip6-…` and `0.0.0.0`) takes each name and `*.` wildcard as the `.` name, the name with its subdomains, as such lists mean it: `HostRange::setWithSubdomains()` on the cache's loading (`TaskZoneDownloader::loadHosts()`), so the cache file doesn't depend on the flag. `TaskZoneDownloader` keeps the normalized names in the Zone's cache file and gives the driver an empty address list, so the Zone is in the driver's `FORT_CONF_ZONES.mask` and `enabled_mask` like the others. Its addresses are added at runtime by `ADDHOSTIPS` (`FORT_CONF_HOST_IPS`: the IPs of a DNS answer with its TTL, to be sent by the service's DNS forwarder): the driver keeps them in `FORT_HOST_IPS` (`fortcnf_host.c`, max 65535 IPs, the expired ones removed when it's full, at most once a minute: the purge walks all IPs under the lock; an IP has one expiration time for all its Zones). An expired IP isn't matched by a new connection, but is matched by an established connection's reauthorization (`conn->is_reauth`, on any conf's change) for a day more (`FORT_HOST_IP_REAUTH_TIME`), as the connections outlive the TTL; it's removed only then. An expired IP added again keeps its old Zones, for the new connections too, till it expires for the reauthorization (as the IP's Zones share its expiration time anyway). The ALE callout looks up the remote IP's Zones once into `conn->host_zones_mask`, which `fort_conf_zones_ip_included()` checks before the Zones' address lists (the Filter Simulator has no such IPs). `SETZONES` removes the IPs' Zones not in its mask, as a removed Zone's id can be reused (`FortManager` writes the Zones on `zoneRemoved`); the client's disconnect doesn't clear the IPs: they stay till their TTL, as the DNS clients' caches keep them.

The Hosts Zones' names are matched in a DB (`HostZonesDb`, `ui/dns/`: `hosts.db` in the cache folder, `FortSettings::hostZonesFilePath()`; opened while the forwarder runs), filled from the Zones' cache files and recreated on its version's change. The table `host(name, name_mask, sub_mask)` has a row per name with the Zones listing the name itself and the Zones listing its subdomains (a name with its subdomains is one row); `host_pattern` has the names with other masks, kept in memory too (`HostPattern`); `host_zone` has each Zone's cache file's checksum (`bin_checksum`) and the `subdomains` flag its names were loaded with. The service's thread, which answers the DNS queries and the RPC, doesn't write it: `DnsManager::syncZones()` passes the Zones' cache files (`TaskInfoZoneDownloader::hostZonesSource()`) to a worker thread (`HostZonesSync`: one job at a time, a sync requested meanwhile runs after it), which opens the DB by its own connection and rewrites only the Zones whose checksum or flag differ (`TaskInfoZoneDownloader::syncHostZone()`, `HostZonesDb::setZone()`, in one transaction per Zone: the matches meanwhile read the last commit, i.e. a Zone's old names till its new ones) and removes the Zones without a cache file or that aren't the Hosts ones anymore; a cache file of another checksum (being rewritten by the Zone's download) keeps the Zone's old names. After the job the service's connection reloads the patterns (`loadPatterns()`); the forwarder's stop waits for the Zone's transaction being written. A disabled Zone's names stay: `match()` returns the enabled Zones (`setEnabledMask()`) of the mask it's given, without a lookup when none of them is; `DnsManager` enables only the Zones having their own names in the DB (`HostZonesFill`): of the Zone's cache file (its checksum and flag) since the DB's open, and then its old names after its update till the commit of the new ones; a Zone's disabling and removal take effect at once (a removed Zone's id can be reused, also while the forwarder is stopped, so the names of another checksum found by the DB's open aren't the Zone's own), its enabling after its names' commit. While an enabled Zone having a cache file but no own names in the DB is filled (the first fill, a new Zone, the recreated DB, a Zone updated or its id reused while the forwarder was stopped), the listener is paused (`DnsListener::setPaused()`: the queries wait in its sockets as with a busy thread, the datagrams in the system's buffer, as an unread one disables Qt's notifications of its socket, the TCP data in Qt's one), so none of its names is answered unblocked; the NRPT check waits for the resume. A name's lookups (the name and its parent domains) run in one read transaction: a statement with a transaction of its own takes several times longer. After a write the DB's page cache is released (`PRAGMA shrink_memory`: it kept ~18 MB after filling 490K names). A test or a tool using the DB needs `Q_INIT_RESOURCE(dns_migrations)`: without the migrations' resource the DB opens with no tables.

The service's DNS forwarder (`ui/dns/`: `DnsManager`, over the `FortFirewallDnsLib` of `src/dns/`; only with `isService()`: without it the DNS tab shows a warning over its options, `OptBasePage::createNoServiceWarning()`; by the conf's `FirewallConf::dnsForwarderEnabled()`, off by default, the Options window's DNS tab's "Filter DNS"; `DnsManager` follows `ConfManager::confChanged`) listens on `127.0.0.1:53` and `[::1]:53`, UDP and TCP (`DnsListener`). While it runs, a rule of the Name Resolution Policy Table (a key of the Dnscache's `Parameters\DnsPolicyConfig`: the namespace `.` to `127.0.0.1`) sends the Dnscache's queries to it; the rule is removed on its stop and on the service's start (e.g. after a crash), and the c-ares sockets don't go through the Dnscache. While the forwarder is enabled with the Service (`dns_forwarder_enabled`, written by `ConfData::writeConfFlags()` by `ConfDriverState::isService`: without the Service the device's client is the UI process; `ConfManager::driverConfState()`: the driver's conf values not kept in `FirewallConf`, with the active Time Periods' mask), the driver allows the Service's connections (`FORT_CONN_REASON_DNS_FORWARDER`) after the flags and the address groups (`fort_conf_conn_app_allowed()`: the traffic is collected; its unknown exe is logged as a new App allowed, without an alert: the App's log record has `FORT_LOG_FLAG_OPT_ALERTED`): the driver keeps the process which opened the device (`fort_device()->client_process_id`) and marks its connections by `conn->is_fort_service`; they and the outbound loopback ones to the forwarder's `127.0.0.1:53` and `[::1]:53` (`fort_conf_conn_loopback_allowed()`: allowed before the conf also with "Filter Local Addresses", else the Dnscache's queries to the forwarder would be asked or blocked, and no name resolved) are permitted hard (`conn->act.permit_hard`: another sublayer's soft block, e.g. a VPN's DNS leak protection, can't override them); the Filter Simulator's connection is the Service's by its "DNS Forwarder" flag (the `simulate` command's `dnsForwarder=1`; a connection of the Connections window by its reason `FORT_CONN_REASON_DNS_FORWARDER`, the log has no other mark of it). Its status (`DnsForwarderStatus`, set by `DnsManager::start()`/`stop()`: off, checking, started, or an error: the NRPT rule not added or ignored while it runs, the Hosts Zones' DB not opened by the start or not written by the last sync while it receives the system's queries (with SQLite's error text: their names aren't matched or are outdated; a failed Zone keeps its old names and is written again by a later sync, `HostZonesDb::errorMessage()`), or no name resolved while an enabled Block Zone having its cache file has no names in the DB (`DnsManager::updateBlockZonesFailed()`: the DB not opened, which isn't retried till the forwarder's restart, or the Zone's fill failed, e.g. its cache file being rewritten by its download, without an error text; the names to block aren't known, so the queries and the answers waiting get `SERVFAIL`), or not started by the mixed or invalid servers or the listener's error, e.g. the port 53 busy, with the socket's error text) is sent to the clients on their init and on its change (`Rpc_DnsManager_updateState`): the DNS tab warns of an error under "Filter DNS" (of the saved options), the control command `dns report` prints it (the exit code 70 + the status); a failed start is retried only on the conf's or the ini's change. After the start it checks that it receives the system's queries (`DnsNrptCheck`: a unique name under `.invalid` resolved by `QHostInfo`, i.e. the Dnscache, its query to the listener answered `NXDOMAIN`; the status is checking till the lookup's end, then started or NRPT ignored, e.g. overridden by the Group Policy's NRPT rules, or the connections to the loopback's port 53 blocked by another WFP filter: a VPN's DNS leak protection (seen with AmneziaVPN: the WFP filters "Block all DNS" with "Allow DNS-Server" permitting only the VPN's DNS servers, in its own sublayer, which cut the listener and the forwarder's upstream queries alike; the ALE callout's permit is soft, so another sublayer's block overrides it: while the forwarder is enabled, the Service's connections and the outbound loopback ones to the forwarder's `127.0.0.1:53` and `[::1]:53` are permitted hard (`conn->act.permit_hard`; the loopback ones by `fort_conf_conn_loopback_allowed()`), i.e. the write right is cleared; whether that wins depends on the sublayers' order and on the other filter's block being hard, a static filter's default); without it the Dnscache asks the adapters' DNS servers, which the blocked ports cut: the DNS tab's warning of the error says so, `DnsPage::forwarderWarningText()`). The conf's `FirewallConf::blockDnsPorts()` (the DNS tab's "Block ports 53 and 853 for other programs", off by default: the tooltip says to turn it on after checking that the names are resolved with DNS filtered) makes the driver block, while the forwarder is enabled (`dns_forwarder_enabled`; the driver doesn't know whether it runs: while it fails, e.g. its port is blocked by a VPN, no name is resolved), the outbound UDP and TCP connections to the remote port 53 (`FORT_CONN_REASON_BLOCK_DNS`) and to the port 853 of DNS over TLS and over QUIC (`FORT_CONN_REASON_BLOCK_DOT`) of the programs other than the Service and of the non-loopback addresses (`block_dns_ports`; `fort_conf_conn_dns_blocked()` after Block All, before the LAN and Internet checks), so they can't resolve the names past the forwarder by these protocols; DNS over HTTPS (port 443 of the web sites, e.g. the browsers' Secure DNS) isn't blocked and bypasses it, as the DNS tab's tooltips say. A query's name listed by an enabled Hosts Zone of the ini's `dns/blockZones` (the DNS tab's "Block Zones": a `HostZonesSelector`, the `ZonesSelector` listing only the Hosts Zones by `MaskSelector::listedMask()`: `ZoneListModel::hostZonesMask()`, by one query; the master clears the bit of a removed Zone, as its id can be reused, and of a Zone whose new source isn't of a Hosts type: `ConfZoneManager::updateDnsBlockZones()`, saved by `ConfManager::saveIniChanged()`, which notifies the clients as the Options window's save does) gets `NXDOMAIN` (with a SOA record for the negative caching) without asking the upstream, even if a `host()` filter lists it (`DnsManager::checkQuery()`); likewise an answer with a CNAME target or an HTTPS/SVCB record's TargetName (of the alias or the service mode; the root `.` is skipped) listed by them (`DnsAnswer::targetNames()`, e.g. a tracker behind the site's subdomain) gets it instead of the upstream's answer, without adding its IPs, as does the answer of a name they list since the query (`DnsManager::matchAnswer()`). The other queries go to the upstream servers of the ini's `dns/servers` (a string list) by the resolver of their protocol (`DnsResolver::create()`, created on the forwarder's start): `DnsPlainResolver` asks the servers' addresses, else the adapters' DNS servers, which c-ares re-reads on the network's changes, by a c-ares channel on its own event thread, `DnsHttpsResolver` the DNS over HTTPS ones (see below); its answer's A/AAAA addresses and the `ipv4hint`/`ipv6hint` ones of its HTTPS/SVCB records (RFC 9460; of the query's name, as a CNAME's addresses, and of the record's target) are added to the Hosts Zones referenced by the driver's conf (`HostRefs::zonesMask()`, see `host()` below: the address groups', Programs' and Rules' Zones; the driver doesn't know the Zones' types, so another Zone's IPs would only fill its table) and the `host()` filters, which list the query's name or a target name of its answer (`DnsManager::matchAnswer()`, as its addresses are of the last canonical name, the hints of the records' targets; by the answer: the Zones and filters may be changed since the query; the Zones' names in the DB, synced with the Zones' cache files on `zoneUpdated` and `TaskManager::zonesUpdated`, a removed Zone's names not matched at once on `zoneRemoved`, and the `host()` filters' names in a `HostMatcher`, rebuilt from `ConfManager::hostRefs()` at once on its `hostRefsChanged`) by `ADDHOSTIPS` (their max TTL plus a margin of 60 s: the programs connect after the answer, a TTL may be 0; a CNAME's shorter TTL doesn't limit it: such a chain is rare, and the programs caching the addresses would lose them sooner) before the answer is sent to the client (`SERVFAIL` if the driver fails). The Dnscache's answers bypass the forwarder: they are flushed (`DnsUtil::flushResolverCache()`) on the forwarder's start, the Block Zones' and the Zones' names' changes and when a `host()` filter's new name or an enabled Hosts Zone is referenced (`DnsManager::updateHostRefs()`: by `HostRefs::addedNamesCount()` and the referenced Zones' mask; a removed reference's names only don't add their IPs anymore), so the names are resolved anew, adding their IPs, and the blocked ones aren't found. The answer is built on the c-ares thread (`DnsAnswer`: the client's id, the upstream's EDNS cookie removed, truncated with `TC` over the client's UDP size) and passed to the service's thread by a queued call. A query from a loopback address with the id and question (the name, type and class) of a query the resolver sent and waits for (`ares_send_dnsrec()`'s qid; c-ares's own cache is off, as a cached answer has no qid) is a loop (the adapters' DNS includes the listener) and gets `SERVFAIL`.

The DNS over HTTPS servers (RFC 8484; `DnsHttpsServer`, `DnsHttpsResolver` of `src/dns/`: a `dns/servers` list of the `https://` URLs; a list mixing them with the plain addresses isn't used: `DnsHttpsServer::isMixedServers()`, `DnsResolver::create()` gives null, the forwarder doesn't start, the DNS tab warns under the servers' field) are URLs with the server's address: as the host, or as the host name's `ip` parameter (`https://dns.example/dns-query?ip=192.0.2.1`, as in Portmaster). A name without the address is refused: the system's resolver would ask the forwarder itself. The query is posted (`application/dns-message`, with the id 0) by `QNetworkAccessManager` to the URL by the address, with the host name as `QNetworkRequest::setPeerVerifyName()`: Schannel sends it as the SNI and verifies the certificate by it, while the `Host` header (HTTP/2's `:authority`) is the address. HTTP/2 needs ALPN (Windows 8.1+): Quad9 and Applied Privacy answer 505 to HTTP/1.1. The servers are asked in the list's order: the next one on an error (of the network or HTTP, a timeout of 4 s, not a DNS response), `SERVFAIL` after the last one, without asking the adapters' servers; a failed server is asked after the other ones for 5 minutes. A redirect isn't followed (its host name would be resolved by the forwarder), the cookies aren't kept. `DnsTest.httpsResolver` asks local HTTP servers (the resolver's `setServers()`).

The DNS tab's combo box sets the servers of a DNS provider (`DnsProvider`, `ui/dns/data/providers.json`: the providers of Portmaster's settings by their DNS over HTTPS URLs) into the servers' field (a server per line; its text is split by `DnsUtil::splitServers()`: by commas, spaces or new lines), which is the option's value: the combo box's index follows the field's servers (0 for the adapters' servers of the empty text, -1 with the "Custom" placeholder for the servers of no provider). After changing the list ask each of its servers by `DnsTest.DISABLED_providersServers` (`DnsTest.exe --gtest_also_run_disabled_tests --gtest_filter=*providersServers`: it needs the network, so it's run only manually). The tab's Test button (only with the service) asks each server of the field for `example.com` by `DnsServersTest` (`src/dns/`: a resolver of the server's protocol per server, in parallel, for 5 s at most; with an invalid server none is asked, with the mixed protocols none is asked and each server's result is `StatusMixed`) on the service: a client's `DnsManagerRpc::createServersTest()` gives a test started by `Rpc_DnsManager_testServers`, each server's result is sent back to the client which started it by `Rpc_DnsManager_serverTested`; the results are shown under the field as they come. The control command `dns test` (`ControlCommandDns`) tests the ini's servers likewise: it sends its result when the test finishes (`ProcessCommandResult::isResultDeferred`, `ControlCommandManager::sendResult()`), the client waits for it longer (`ControlCommandBase::resultWaitMsecs()`); a server not answering NOERROR makes it an error.

The Connections' host names are the names the programs asked (`conn.remote_host`, by the conn DBs' `LogConnJob`; the Connections windows' "Show host names" shows it in the remote address's column, else the address's PTR name of `HostInfoCache`; `conn list`'s `host`): the master's `DnsClientManager` listens to the DNS Client's ETW events (`DnsEtwListener` of `src/dns/`, the event 3008 of Microsoft-Windows-DNS-Client, also for the Dnscache's cached answers; Windows 7 has no such event) and keeps the answer's name by its process id (the client's, `EventHeader.ProcessId`) and each address (`DnsClientAnswer`: the IPv4 addresses come mapped to IPv6) for an hour after the last answer, as the event has no TTL and the programs keep the addresses in their own caches and connections' pools (a process's id reused meanwhile gets the name of the same address only); `LogManager::processLogEntryConn()` takes it by the connection's process id and remote address (`ProcessIpKey`), else the name of the service's DNS forwarder's last answer with the address (`DnsManager::addConnHostName()`: to any process, as the forwarder answers the Dnscache; kept by the process id 0 for the answer's TTL plus the 60 s margin of `ADDHOSTIPS`), which comes before the connection, so its row is stored named at once (a crash before the event doesn't lose the name); a CDN's address of several names may get another one of them till the process's event. The events are delivered by the session's flush timer (100 ms on Windows 8+, `EVENT_TRACE_USE_MS_FLUSH_TIMER`; else 1 s), the driver's log every 500 ms: a process's first connection is usually logged before its answer's event (each process gets its own event, the cached answers too). So `StatConnManager` (and `PendingConnManager`) keeps a connection logged without its process's name waiting 5 s (`m_hostWaits`: the key and the newest conn id before it), and `DnsClientManager::hostNameAdded` (a new or another name of the key; not of the forwarder's answers) names its rows by `UpdateConnHostsJob` (`remote_host IS NOT` the name after that id: the forwarder's name is replaced); its `connHostsChanged(connIdMin)` (by RPC too; only when it changed a row) makes the models re-read the names: `ConnListModel` refreshes its rows, the search models (`ConnSearchModel`, the Alert Program window's `AppPendingConnSearchModel` with its listed rows) set the names of their loaded rows after the id (`loadConnHosts()`). The search matches the stored name (`connRowSearchText()`), not the PTR one; a new row unmatched before its name came isn't matched again by the name (till the next search). The "Add Filter" and "Copy as Filter" add the name as `Host()` beside the remote IP (`FilterLineText::addConnAddresses()`: in one line they both must match).

The connections opened before the service's start and logged again get the names of the previous run's live flows: `ActivityManager::restoreFlowHosts()` reads the old Activity DB's `flow.remote_host`s before recreating it, not of a run before the boot (`OsUtil::bootTime()`: the processes' ids are reused), and `DnsClientManager::addFlowHost()` keeps them by the process id as the answers' names. The names `DnsClientManager` keeps are written on the stop to the cache folder's `dns-hosts.bin` (`FortSettings::dnsHostsFilePath()`, `DnsHostsFile`: a header with the write time, then sections of a tag and a size, which a reader skips when unknown, so a later version adds its data as a new tag's section) and read back on the start with their remaining times, all or none, not of a run before the boot; a crash loses them.

A rule text's `host(example.com, *.cdn.net)` filter (`FORT_RULE_FILTER_TYPE_HOST`; its names as in `HostRange`; an address type: `host(a.com):443` is its port) matches the remote IPs of its names' DNS answers, also of the answers with a CNAME target or an HTTPS/SVCB record's TargetName listed by it. Each name of a filter, as `HostRange::toList()` lists it (`example.com`; `*.example.org` of a `*.` wildcard or of a `.` name; a pattern), has an id: the first 8 bytes of its SHA-256 (`HostRange::nameId()`; 0 is of the Zones' IPs), so a name has the same id in any filter, after an edit of the filter's other names and after the service's restart; the driver's filter has the sorted ids of its names (`FORT_CONF_RULE_FILTER_HOST`, `HostRange::nameIds()`), not the names. A collision of two names' ids isn't checked: of n names its chance is about n²/2^65. The master's `ConfManager::hostRefs()` keeps the sets of names of the Rules' and the Programs' filters (`HostRefs::namesKey()`: the sorted normalized names), which `ConfBuffer` with the references (`setHostRefs()`) collects while writing the Rules (`SETRULES`), the Programs (`SETCONF`) or a Program (`ADDAPP`), and the writes forget the sets neither the Rules nor the Programs have (`HostRefs::Source`; a Program's names and Zones are kept by its path, as the driver keeps the Programs: its `ADDAPP` replaces them, of its deletion too); without them (validation, the Filter Simulator) the buffer collects into its own: the Filter Simulator doesn't know the names' addresses: `FilterSimulator` filters the connection again as if the remote IP were of all host() filters' names (`HostRefs::nameIds()`, by `DriverCommon::ConnFilterConf::hostNameIds`) and Hosts Zones (`ZonesData::hostsMask` as `host_zones_mask`), and a changed decision sets `FilterSimConn::hostDependent`: the window notes that the driver's result may differ, `simulate` prints `hostDependent`. A filter negating a name (`!host()`) may keep the decision of both runs, while the driver's differs. With a host name (`FilterSimConn::hostName`, Punycode by `QUrl::toAce()`) the connection is filtered as the IP were of the name's answer instead: `DnsManager::matchName()` (of the service, while the forwarder receives the system's queries, else `HostNameNoForwarder`: the driver gets no names' addresses) gives the referenced Hosts Zones and the host() filters' names listing it, or its Block Zones (`HostNameBlocked`: not filtered, the Block Zone as `act.zone_id`). Its answer's CNAME targets and HTTPS/SVCB TargetNames, which the forwarder matches too, aren't known: the window notes it. `simulate` takes it as `host=` and prints `hostName` (`none`, `matched`, `blocked`, `noForwarder`). Likewise `HostRefs::zonesMask()` has the Zones the driver's conf references: the address groups' include and exclude Zones and the Programs' Zones of `SETCONF` and `ADDAPP`, the Rules' Zones of `SETRULES` (`HostRefs::addZones()`); a change of the filters or the Zones emits `ConfManager::hostRefsChanged`, and `HostRefs::addedNamesCount()` grows on a name no filter had. `ADDHOSTIPS` adds the IPs to the Zones and to the ids of the filters' names listing the answer's names (`FORT_CONF_HOST_IPS.name_n` ids before the IPs, by `HostMatcher`): `FORT_HOST_IPS` keeps a name's IP as the IP and `name_id` entry with its own expiration time (the Zones' IP has the id 0), hashed by the IP alone, so the filter's check (the connection's `host_ip_included` callback with its `host_ip_ctx`, under the table's lock; the Filter Simulator's one looks up the ids of `DriverCommon::ConnFilterConf::hostNameIds`, without them it has none) walks the IP's entries in its bucket and looks up their ids in the filter's ones (`fort_conf_host_name_included()`). The IPs aren't cleared on the device's open: the restarted service's filters have the same ids, so an established connection allowed by a filter keeps matching on the reauthorization, also after an edit of the filter's other names; a removed name's IPs only stay unmatched till they expire.
