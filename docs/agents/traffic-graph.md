# Traffic graph

Moved out of `AGENTS.md`: read it when working on this area.

The traffic graph (`form/graph/`) has no 3rd-party chart library: `GraphView` (the graph's `IniGraph`, its colors, fonts and format, the right click's tray menu) feeds `StatManager::trafficAdded` into `GraphPlot`, a `QGraphicsView` whose scene items (grid, axes, tick labels, in/out bars, text speed) are laid out in the viewport's pixels; `GraphWindow` is its floating host (the window's flags, opacity and Hide on hover, dragging and the sticky screen edges, the geometry, the speed in the title by `GraphView::speedUpdated()`). It keeps the per-second points itself (a delayed second is merged, not cleared) and is replotted once per second by `GraphView`'s timer, not while hidden; the axes are re-laid out only when the value range, the size or the tick labels change. The text speed is placed by the Speed box's "Position" (`IniGraph::speedPosition()`, `GraphSpeedPosition`: at the top or bottom in a line, at the left or right in two rows of the widest text's width) inside the plot area over the graph, or without "Inside the graph" (`IniGraph::speedInside()`) in a margin of the axis rectangle (`GraphPlot::speedMargins()`, kept as laid out), centered on the graph along the side; the text alone (the graph hidden) is in the view's center. The axis lines at the graph's sides are shown by the "Axis lines" check boxes (`IniGraph::axisLines()`, a mask of `GraphAxisLine`'s bits, only the left base line by default), independent of the speed and the ticks: between the graph and the outside speed or the ticks they are in its margin, else the right and the bottom ones take a pixel of the graph's side (`GraphPlot::axisLineWidth()`; the bottom one is the X axis line, which the bars stand on), the left one is on the view's edge. The text's plate (the background's color, half transparent) is drawn only inside the graph: outside it would only make a translucent background more opaque under the text. Between the replots the bars scroll by a device pixel (a second is the "Bar width" option's pixels (5 by default) rounded to the device pixels, the last second at the right edge; the bars are laid out in device pixels too, widths and heights, so they don't jump or gap with a fractional scale like 125%; a second's in and out bars overlap, the smaller one in front, their sum above in the "Total" color; the "Show" options hide the Total, Download and Upload graphs (the Total by default: a hidden one's bars are empty, its line is hidden; the Total line's area is filled by a gradient, without it the Download and Upload ones are, by fill-only `GraphLineItem`s below both lines) and the value range is by the visible ones); the last complete second's bars rise and the value range's change is animated (`GraphAnimation`, ~30 frames per second, not `QVariantAnimation`'s ~60), so the view keeps the default minimal viewport update. The bars are drawn by `GraphBarsItem` as rectangles (`QPainter::drawRects()`): filling a `QPainterPath` of them is ~60× slower. The "Graph type" option (`IniGraph::graphType()`) switches the bars to the Line type: `GraphLineItem`'s monotone cubic curves of 2 device pixels, drawn as antialiased cosmetic lines of a device pixel with offsets (a thick antialiased line is ~10× slower); the lines have no rising points. The bars and the lines are `GraphCachedItem`s: painted to a pixmap aligned to the device pixels on their change, the scrolled ones only draw it (`QGraphicsItem::DeviceCoordinateCache` is slower: it's regenerated on the scroll). Without traffic nothing is scrolled, animated or repainted. The scroll and the animations are switched off by the graph's "Animation" option (`IniGraph::animation()`, `GraphPlot::setAnimated()`): then the graph is repainted once per second.

There are up to 8 graphs (`IniGraph::maxCount`), each a `GraphWindow` or a `TaskbarGraphWindow` (both `GraphBaseWindow`s of a `GraphView`) of its id with its options in the `.user.ini` group of `IniGraph::groupName()`: `[graphWindow]` for the main graph (id 0, always present, as before the graphs' list), `[graphWindow1]`…`[graphWindow7]` for the extra ones, listed by `graphsWindow/graphIds` (`IniUser::graphWindowIds()`). Their `FormPointer`s are of the same `WindowGraph` code with the graph's id: the main one is `WindowManager`'s form of the code, the extra ones are in `m_graphForms` (`graphFormById()`); only the main one is tracked by the code (`windowOpened()`/`windowClosed()`: the tray's "Traffic Graph" action of the Graphs menu and its hot key, `-c graph` and `-c window graph` show the main graph), all of them emit `graphWindowVisibilityChanged()`, which checks the extra graphs' actions of the tray's Graphs menu (`TrayIcon::updateGraphActions()`, by their names, after the main graph's "Traffic Graph"). The main graph's name is always "Traffic Graph" (`GraphTypes::graphName()`, read-only in `GraphEditDialog`). The Graphs window (`GraphsWindow`, `WindowGraphs`, password-protected, not dockable, opened by the tray's Graphs menu) lists them by `GraphListModel` (its check box shows or closes the graph's window) and adds, edits and removes them: `GraphEditDialog` edits a copy of the `IniUser` by `GraphOptionsArea` (the former Options' Traffic Graph tab) and saves it by `GraphsController::saveGraph()` on OK and on Apply (enabled by `GraphOptionsArea::edited()` and the name's edit); the removal deletes the window and the ini group (`MapSettings::removeGroup()`).

## The taskbar's graphs

A graph's "Show in taskbar" (`IniGraph::showInTaskbar()`, the Window box's Taskbar part with its "Width", `taskbarWidth()` at 100% scale) makes `FormPointer` create a `TaskbarGraphWindow` instead of a `GraphWindow`: the floating window's options (geometry, opacity, Hide on hover, dragging, the title's speed) don't apply to it. A new `.user.ini` gets an extra graph "Taskbar" shown in the taskbar (`IniUser::addDefaultTaskbarGraph()`). A window of another type or layering than needed (`FormWindow::isHostChanged()`: the option toggled, or the taskbar's mode changed while it was hidden) is deleted and created anew on its showing (`FormPointer::show()`, `WindowManager::updateGraphWindowHost()`/`recreateGraphWindow()`); a visible one is recreated on `TaskbarHost::windowsInvalidated()` by a queued call (it is deleted in it).

The light or the dark colors of any graph are chosen by the Colors box's "Theme" (`IniGraph::theme()`, `GraphTheme`): the default "System" follows the app's color scheme for a window, but the system's light/dark theme, not the apps' one, for a graph in the taskbar (`GraphView`'s `isDarkSystemTheme()`; before Windows 10 the taskbar is light, `OsUtil::isSystemLightTheme()`).

`TaskbarHost` (`form/graph/`, one in `WindowManager::taskbarHost()`, plain Win32) places the shown windows into the primary monitor's taskbar (`Shell_TrayWnd`; the secondary ones' aren't supported) as a chain from the notification area (`TrayNotifyWnd`), ordered by the graph id (`WindowOptions::order`: graph 0 the nearest). Its mode is chosen by the taskbar's windows, not the Windows version, when the first window is added and again on the taskbar's change:
- `ModeTaskList` (Windows 7/10, a classic taskbar like StartAllBack's or ExplorerPatcher's: `MSTaskSwWClass`'s `MSTaskListWClass` visible): the windows are children of `ReBarWindow32`, in the room made by shrinking `MSTaskSwWClass` once by the windows' total length (`m_shrunkListWidth`/`m_shrunkWidth`: the task list of the width set by us is our layout, else the Explorer's; its height and position aren't compared, as the Explorer changes them keeping our width. A list reaching the rebar's end is the Explorer's even of that width, as its full one may equal ours; the width is still compared, as other bands may follow the list), so any hiding order gives its width back; it is moved by `SWP_ASYNCWINDOWPOS`, not waiting for the Explorer's thread. As such a move may be still pending, the layout on the taskbar's change is deferred till 200 ms after the Explorer's last one (`scheduleLayout()`): else our previous width of the list would be taken as the Explorer's one and shrunk again (e.g. on the taskbar buttons' size change). A killed Fort leaves it shrunk till the Explorer's re-layout.
- `ModeTaskbar` (the Windows 11 taskbar: it keeps an empty `MSTaskSwWClass` with its list hidden): children of `Shell_TrayWnd` left of the notification area, over its content: the centered icons may lie under them (a click on an alpha-0 pixel reaches them).
- `ModeOverlay` (the vertical taskbar, or `SetParent()` failed): topmost top-level windows over the taskbar, along it up to the task list's bottom (the language bar's band below stays clear).

Embedding is by Qt: `windowHandle()->setParent(QWindow::fromWinId())` of the foreign window (deleted without the children: it would delete them), unembedded on hiding. A window not accepting the focus swallows `WM_SETFOCUS` (`TaskbarGraphWindow::nativeEvent()`): within `SetParent()` Qt would re-activate the other graph's window with a `requestActivate()` warning.

The windows are layered (`WA_TranslucentBackground`: Qt flushes them by `UpdateLayeredWindowIndirect(ULW_ALPHA)`, the Background color's alpha per pixel, transparent by default) when `canLayerWindows()`: the overlay always, the children on Windows 8+ only, and only with the exe manifest's Windows 8+ `supportedOS` (`ui_bin`'s `QMAKE_MANIFEST`), else `SetLayeredWindowAttributes()` fails with 87. Windows 11 shows only a layered child in its taskbar: a plain one's GDI goes under the `DesktopWindowContentBridge`'s DComp visual. Windows 7's children are opaque. A mode change switching the layering invalidates the windows (`windowsInvalidated()`).

Placement in device pixels: Qt keeps a window's geometry in device-independent pixels and paints a layered window at the native position rounded to them and back (a coordinate of 5n + 2 moves by 1 at 125%), so the windows' spans are of "native exact" coordinates (`nativeExactSpan()` along the bar, flush with the notification area and each other; `crossSpan()` across it: the most of the taskbar's visible part, out of the screen's or the parent's edge by up to `ceil(dpr)`, never into the area beside the taskbar; at 125% a 1 px strip of the right vertical taskbar may stay uncovered). After its `SetWindowPos()` the widget's geometry is synced (`syncWidgetGeometry()`): Qt's native show after the show event applies the widget's own size otherwise (the layout's `SetNoConstraint` keeps its minimum out).

Events: WinEvent hooks out of context, `EVENT_OBJECT_LOCATIONCHANGE` and `EVENT_SYSTEM_FOREGROUND` on the taskbar's thread (re-layout, the edge's change by `updateMode()`), `EVENT_OBJECT_REORDER` of all processes (the desktop's z-order: the overlay is raised when the taskbar is above it, walking `GetWindow()` by up to `maxZOrderSteps`). The Start menu moves the taskbar into another z-band (`GetWindowBand()` 6, out of the `GetWindow()` chain) and Explorer returns it after the menu's closing on top of the topmost windows: only a raise after that return holds, hence the REORDER hook, not the foreground one. Explorer's restart: the `TaskbarCreated` registered message, got by a hidden `WS_POPUP` window (a message-only one doesn't get broadcasts; `ChangeWindowMessageFilterEx()` for an elevated Fort), invalidates the windows (the embedded ones are destroyed with the old taskbar; `addWindow()` skips a dead native window). Windows 10 may send it ~60 s after the restart: till then the embedded graphs are gone.
